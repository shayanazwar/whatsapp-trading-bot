# Backtesting methodology

The event-driven backtester iterates through the historical 1H candle stream chronologically. A signal decision is evaluated only after the current 1H candle closes. Each 1D, 12H and 4H context is filtered so its full candle close is no later than the decision timestamp. The hypothetical entry is the next 1H candle open, not the signal candle close.

The engine applies contract-specific contract size and taker fees, configured slippage, and historical funding events. All simulated entries/exits are treated as taker fills unless a separate maker-fill model is implemented; no maker-fee configuration is exposed. Funding uses the event fair price where present, otherwise the last completed 1H close is used as an explicitly labelled proxy. Funding exposure is reduced by the configured TP1 exit fraction only for settlements strictly after the completed candle that confirms TP1; settlements at the same candle-close timestamp are conservatively charged on the full original position. If historical funding data is unavailable, malformed or cannot be valued, the backtest fails closed instead of silently assuming zero funding. When stop and target are both touched in one OHLC bar, the simulator uses a conservative stop-first assumption. Stop-market gaps are filled at the adverse candle open. Positions still open at the end are marked separately and are not counted as closed wins.

The default exit schedule closes 50% at TP1=1R and the remaining 50% at TP2=2.5R. Partial exits are accounted as two separate quantity legs, including the second-leg loss when the stop is hit after TP1. Fees, slippage, funding and net R use this same weighted schedule. Mark-to-market drawdown includes hourly close and conservative intrabar-adverse marks, but it is still an OHLC estimate, not exchange tick-level account equity.

The current event engine is single-symbol per job and uses the current contract metadata. Historical point-in-time contract metadata, order-book queue replay, partial depth fills, market impact and full portfolio/cross-margin liquidation are not modeled. This limitation must remain visible in every interpretation. The backtest can optionally apply a user-supplied historical Kronos filter callback; actual full rolling Kronos walk-forward backtests require model inference at each historical origin and sufficient compute and are expensive. Never reuse future forecast outputs as earlier backtest features.
